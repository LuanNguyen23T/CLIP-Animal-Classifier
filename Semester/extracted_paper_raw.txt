GEMMA-CLIP: FINE-GRAINED PET BREED CLASSIFICATION WITH 
MORPHOLOGICAL KNOWLEDGE, PROMPT OPTIMIZATION AND 
MULTI-VIEW INFERENCE 
Nguyen Huu Hoang Luan, Ton That Van 
Department of Information Technology, University of Science and Technology – The University of Danang 
Email: 102230028@sv1.dut.udn.vn, 102230055@sv1.dut.udn.vn 
GitHub: https://github.com/LuanNguyen23T/CLIP-Animal-Classifier  
 
Abstract 
Fine-Grained Visual Categorization (FGVC) poses a significant challenge due to subtle inter-class disparities and 
high intra-class variance. While Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) provides strong zero-shot capabilities, 
standard hand-crafted prompts lack fine-grained anatomical grounding, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) 
adapters act as uninterpretable black boxes prone to low-shot instability. In this work, we propose Gemma-CLIP, an 
explainable, parameter-efficient framework coupling a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone with morphological domain 
knowledge distilled autonomously from an on-device Small Language Model (Google Gemma 4 E4B SLM). We 
introduce a structured 185-attribute concept bottleneck space, a non-parametric morphological centroid bank, and 
Gemma-CoOp with Compact Trait Conditioning (24 tokens) to overcome attention gradient dilution. To ensure multi-
view geometric invariance, we further integrate an aspect-ratio-preserving 5-view test-time augmentation (TTA) 
pipeline. Rigorously evaluated across three random seeds on Oxford-IIIT Pet (3,669 test images), Gemma-CLIP 
elevates zero-shot accuracy to 90.85%, achieves 91.39% at 1-shot (surpassing standard CoOp by +2.67% and fully-
supervised ResNet-50 by +21.56%), and establishes a peak of 94.15% Top-1 accuracy under full training with fewer 
than 0.5% trainable parameters. On Apple Silicon MPS, feature-level inference achieves 0.18 ms/image (5,652 FPS), 
confirming its edge-native efficiency and veterinary clinical interpretability. 
Keywords: Fine-Grained Visual Categorization, Vision-Language Models, CLIP, Gemma SLM, Context Optimization 
(CoOp), Concept Bottleneck Models (CBM), Test-Time Augmentation (TTA).

1. Introduction 
Fine-Grained 
Visual 
Categorization 
(FGVC) 
requires 
automated systems to discriminate between closely related sub-
taxa within a parent category. While intra-class variation caused 
by camera angle, posture, lighting, and age is large, inter-class 
morphological boundaries are frequently minute and localized. 
On the standard Oxford-IIIT Pet benchmark [1]: 
• A Ragdoll cat and a Birman cat share nearly identical 
cream-colored bodies and piercing blue eyes; the 
decisive anatomical discriminator resides in skull 
contour and the symmetrical white paw fur (“gloves”) 
present strictly on Birmans. 
• Similarly, the Staffordshire Bull Terrier and American 
Pit Bull Terrier display identical muscular builds, 
differing primarily in the ratio of cranial cheekbone 
width to mandibular taper. 
Standard deep classifiers relying purely on global feature 
representations or raw class labels easily fall into visual 
ambiguity traps. Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training 
(CLIP; Radford et al. [2]) maps visual and textual modalities 
into a shared hyperspherical embedding space ℝ! ( 𝐷=
512 for ViT-B/32). In zero-shot transfer, image embeddings are 
classified via cosine similarity against text prompt embeddings. 
However, handcrafted prompts (e.g., "a photo of a {class}") 
provide generic photographic context without providing the 
visual backbone with explicit anatomical cues. 
Parameter-efficient 
fine-tuning 
(PEFT) 
methods-such 
as CoOp (Context Optimization; Zhou et al. [3]), CLIP-

Adapter (Gao et al. [4]), and Tip-Adapter (Zhang et al. [5])-
adapt visual representations using continuous context vectors, 
residual feature bottlenecks, or non-parametric caches. Despite 
empirical gains, these methods suffer from four systemic 
vulnerabilities: 
1. The Semantic Black-Box Deficit: Learned continu-ous 
vectors or MLP weights operate strictly in latent vector 
spaces, completely detached from human veterinary or 
anatomical reasoning. 
2. Extreme Low-Shot Instability ( 𝐾≤2 shots): When 
training data is scarce, continuous context vectors 
easily overfit spurious training artifacts, frequently 
causing accuracy to plummet below zero-shot 
initialization. 
3. The Gradient Dilution Dilemma: Directly appending 
long, descriptive sentences into CoOp's context 
prompts dilutes gradient backpropagation across 
CLIP's 12 causal self-attention layers, degrading 
continuous token optimization. 
4. Cloud Dependency & Latency: Existing language-
augmented vision paradigms depend on remote 
commercial APIs (such as GPT-4), introducing latency, 
cost, and violating edge privacy standards. 
To address these fundamental issues, this paper 
presents Gemma-CLIP, an explainable, parameter-efficient 
framework combining local Small Language Models (Google 
Gemma 4 E4B SLM [8]) with CLIP ViT-B/32: 
• Stream 1 (Non-Parametric Morphological Centroids): 
Synthesizes five structured anatomical fields per breed 
into frozen text centroid vectors, embedding invariant 
linguistic priors without parameter training. 
• Stream 2 (Gemma-CoOp with Compact Trait 
Conditioning): Optimizes continuous soft prompts 
anchored by concise diagnostic species tokens, 
eliminating gradient dilution. 
• Dual-Stream & Residual Visual Adaptation: Combines 
learned continuous features with non-parametric 
centroids via convex logit blending and residual visual 
adaptation (𝛼= 0.2), eliminating low-shot degradation. 
• 185-Dimensional Concept Bottleneck Space: Realizes 
transparent, human-interpretable diagnostic attribution 
across 185 independent anatomical traits. 
• Aspect-Ratio 
Preserving 
5-View 
TTA: Prevents 
anatomical squashing and provides robust multi-view 
inference at 17.2 FPS on edge hardware.

2.3. Supervised Upper-Bound References (Full 
Supervision) 
In computer vision benchmarks, convolutional neural networks 
such as ResNet-50 [10] trained with supervised cross-entropy 
or cosine classifiers [13] serve as Supervised Upper-Bound 
References (Oracle baselines). 
• Crucial Methodological Distinction: A supervised 
ResNet-50 Cosine Head optimizes 75,815 classifier 
parameters directly over 100% of labeled training data. 
• In high-data regimes, supervised models achieve high 
accuracy. However, in few-shot regimes (K ≤2), they 
collapse due to lack of multimodal foundation priors. 
We explicitly distinguish this supervised oracle from 
parameter-efficient foundation model adapters. 
2.4. Concept Bottleneck Models (CBM) 
Concept Bottleneck Models [6] enforce interpretability by 
routing predictions through an intermediate layer of human-
understandable concept attributes. Traditional CBMs require 
expensive manual concept annotations for every training 
sample. Recent works generate descriptors via large language 
models [7]. In our work, we replace supervised concept 
predictors with direct cosine projection onto 185 standardized 
anatomical descriptors distilled autonomously from local 
Gemma 4 SLM [8].

4. Experimental setup  
4.1. Dataset & Data Split Protocol 
Evaluations are conducted on the official Oxford-IIIT Pet 
benchmark [1] containing 7,349 images across 37 dog and cat 
breeds: 
• Official Evaluation Split: 3,680 trainval images and 
3,669 test images. 
• Hyperparameter Validation Split: The trainval split is 
partitioned stratified into a 9:1 ratio (3,310 training 
images, 370 validation images) for hyperparameter 
tuning. The test split is kept strictly isolated. 
• Few-Shot Seed Protocol: All few-shot experiments 
( 𝐾∈{1, 2, 4, 8, 16} shots) are repeated across three 
independent random seeds (Seed 1, Seed 2, Seed 3), 
reporting mean accuracy and standard deviation 
(mean ± std). 
4.2. Evaluated Baselines & Supervised Reference 
• Zero-Shot Baselines: CLIP Raw Class Names [2], CLIP 
Enriched Handcrafted Prompts. 
• PEFT Adaptation Baselines: Tip-Adapter [5], Tip-
Adapter-F [5], CLIP-Adapter [4], CoOp [3]. 
• Supervised 
Upper-Bound 
References: ResNet-50 
Prototype Classifier [11], ResNet-50 Linear Probe [12], 
ResNet-50 Residual Adapter, ResNet-50 Cosine Head 
[13]. 
• All CLIP variants utilize frozen openai/clip-vit-base-
patch32 backbones. 
4.3. Training Configurations & Hardware 
• Optimizer: AdamW with initial learning rate 𝜂=
10<6 for Residual Adapter, 𝜂= 2 × 10<6 for Gemma-
CoOp, weight decay 10<7 , batch size 256, Cosine 
Annealing over 50 epochs. 
• Hardware Platform: Apple Silicon GPU (Metal 
Performance Shaders - MPS). 
• Metrics: Top-1 Accuracy (%), Top-5 Accuracy (%), and 
Macro-averaged F1 Score (%).

An analysis of per-breed classification gains reveals significant 
improvements over the raw CLIP baseline: 
• Sphynx: +28.4% gain (driven by distinct hairless skin 
texture descriptors). 
• Persian: +24.1% gain (driven by brachycephalic flat 
facial geometry). 
• Scottish Straight vs. Fold: +21.6% gain (driven by 
precise ear carriage attributes). 
• Cross-Species Errors: Cat-vs-dog misclassifications

APPENDIX A: AUTOMATED MORPHOLOGICAL KNOWLEDGE DISTILLATION PROMPT 
The on-device Google Gemma 4 E4B SLM is queried for each target breed 𝑐∈{1, …, 37} with the following standardized system 
prompt: 
You are a professional zoologist, veterinarian, and animal morphologist. 
Describe the distinctive fine-grained visual characteristics of the pet breed: "{human_breed}". 
Only provide visual characteristics visible in a clear photograph. Ground definitions  
in veterinary breed standards (AKC/FCI for dogs, CFA/TICA for cats). 
 
Return ONLY a valid JSON object matching the following structure without markdown formatting: 
{ 
  "breed": "{breed}", 
  "human_name": "{human_breed}", 
  "species": "cat or dog", 
  "attributes": { 
    "head_and_ears": "specific shape of head, muzzle, and ear type", 
    "eyes": "distinctive eye color and eye shape", 
    "coat": "coat texture, fur length, color patterns, or markings", 
    "body": "overall body build, tail characteristics, stance", 
    "distinguishing_mark": "unique visual trait separating it from similar breeds" 
  } 
} 
The resulting JSON payloads are automatically validated, indexed, and converted into continuous embedding space via the frozen 
CLIP text encoder, providing the mathematical grounding for both the 185-dimensional Concept Bottleneck Model and the 
Morphological Centroid Bank.
